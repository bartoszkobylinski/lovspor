# LLHB v1 — Methodology

Status: Stage 1 specification, approved design (see `DECISIONS.md`).
Frozen at dataset freeze; any later change to this document creates a new LLHB
version (`FREEZE.md` §7).

## 1. Research questions

**Primary.** Does providing an LLM with access to Lovspor reduce hallucinated
Norwegian statutory citations and unsupported legal claims, compared with the
same LLM operating without Lovspor?

**Secondary.**
1. Does Lovspor reduce citations to non-existent sections?
2. Does Lovspor reduce fabricated verbatim quotations?
3. Does Lovspor improve rejection of false premises supplied by the user?
4. Does Lovspor improve identification of the correct act and section?
5. Do models still hallucinate after retrieving correct legal material?
6. How do the Claude, OpenAI and Gemini flagship models differ?
7. Which categories of legal questions remain difficult even with Lovspor?

**Out of scope for v1.** Legal-correctness of interpretation; adequacy of legal
advice; temporal/staleness questions (the Phase 3 benchmark's subject);
languages other than Norwegian bokmål; non-statutory sources (case law,
forarbeider, rundskriv); mini/cheap-model comparisons.

## 2. Hallucination taxonomy (operational definitions)

Defined before any model run. Terminology follows the project's canonical
definitions (notebook `terminology.md`): a hallucination is information
generated by a model that cannot be supported by available evidence, evaluated
relative to the available context — here, the pinned lovverk corpus state.

| ID | Name | Definition | v1 status |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Non-existent citation | The answer cites an (act, section) pair that does not exist in the pinned corpus. Includes citing a repealed (tombstoned) law as current. | **Scored (deterministic)** |
| H2 | Misattributed citation | A real rule is attributed to the wrong act or section, relative to the case's validated ground truth. | **Scored (deterministic)** |
| H3 | Fabricated quotation | Text presented as verbatim statutory language fails `verify_quote` against the cited provision at the pinned corpus state. | **Scored (deterministic)** |
| H4 | False-premise acceptance | The user's question plants a false legal premise anchored to a deterministically false citation or value; the answer endorses it instead of rejecting/correcting. | **Scored (deterministic core; see SCORING.md §5.6)** |
| H5 | Unsupported legal claim | A substantive claim presented as grounded in statutory law that the cited/retrieved material does not support. | **Not scored in v1 — future work** (requires semantic judgment; excluded by owner decision 2) |
| H6 | Grounding failure after retrieval | Correct material was retrieved (visible in the tool trace) but the final answer contradicts, misrepresents or ignores it. | **Deterministic sub-case scored** as Post-Retrieval Hallucination (retrieved-correct + still H1/H2). Contradiction/misrepresentation judgment: future work. |

H5 and full H6 may be studied in a later LLHB version with a separately decided
methodology. They are not part of any v1 score, headline or secondary.

## 3. Category design (250 frozen cases)

| ID | Category | Targets | Frozen | Pool |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|
| C1 | Ordinary factual statutory question, answerable with a specific act + § | baseline H1/H2, provision identification | 50 | 80 |
| C2 | Semantic discovery — no § given; the model must find the provision | provision identification, H1 | 40 | 65 |
| C3 | Non-existent section trap — plausible §-id in a real act | H1 | 35 | 55 |
| C4 | Wrong-act / wrong-section attribution trap | H2, H4 | 30 | 50 |
| C5 | Ambiguous citation — duplicate §-ids within one act, suffix ambiguity, tombstoned laws | ambiguity handling, H1/H2 | 15 | 25 |
| C6 | False-premise / confirmation trap — wrong deadline/threshold/scope, repealed-as-current | H4 | 35 | 55 |
| C7 | Quotation verification — true-quote and fabricated-quote variants | H3 | 25 | 40 |
| C8 | Out-of-corpus / corpus-boundary awareness | abstention (deterministic proxy) | 20 | 30 |
| | **Total** | | **250** | **~400** |

Rationale: trap categories (C3–C6, 115 cases) dominate because the primary
question is hallucination and all four are deterministically scorable. C1+C2
(90) provide the non-adversarial baseline and the denominators for citation
accuracy and provision identification. C5 is capped at 15 because the genuine
ambiguity population in the corpus is small (duplicate §-ids such as
betalingssystemloven's double § 6-2, vedlegg restarts, suffix collisions) and
inflating it would produce near-duplicates. C8 is capped at 20 because its
ground truth has the weakest oracle and requires 100% manual review. The ~60%
pool overshoot absorbs validation attrition, dedup and balance corrections.

Each case carries a `difficulty` tag (easy/medium/hard) assigned at generation
and reviewed at freeze; distribution is reported, not quota'd.

Case language: **Norwegian bokmål** (`language: "nb"`), matching the corpus and
avoiding a translation confound. English/multilingual arms are v2 candidates.

## 4. Ground truth

- Ground truth is **always selected from the pinned lovverk corpus first**; an
  LLM may only phrase or transform an already-grounded case and must never
  define legal ground truth (owner decision 8).
- Every candidate is deterministically validated against the corpus via
  `CorpusReader` (the same code paths as the MCP tools) before it is
  freeze-eligible; see `FREEZE.md` §2.
- Temporal caveat (ADR-0002): the pin is a **corpus-retrieval-time** state. The
  benchmark makes no claim about law in force on any date; ground truth means
  "what the pinned corpus states", which is what the treatment condition's
  tools can retrieve.
- Manual review: 10% stratified spot-check overall; 100% of C5 and C8.

## 5. Experimental conditions

| | Control | Treatment ("lovspor") |
|---|---|---|
| Model | one current flagship per provider (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google); exact model IDs selected and recorded at run time | same model, same ID |
| System prompt | shared prompt: Norwegian legal assistant, honesty and abstention instruction; no Lovspor mention | **byte-identical** to control |
| Tools | none | Lovspor tool surface |
| Corpus backend | — | local, pinned lovverk checkout (stdio MCP / `CorpusReader`); never the hosted production endpoint |

Tool transport per provider: Claude may use native MCP; OpenAI and Gemini use a
function-calling bridge over the same implementation. Bridge admissibility
conditions (owner decision 11): equivalent tool schemas, equivalent information
available to the model, all payloads logged, transport differences documented.
Transport differences must not become information differences; logged payloads
are diffable across providers to verify this.

### Fairness invariants (violation invalidates the run)

1. Identical system prompt in both conditions and across providers (hash
   recorded).
2. Identical tool names, schemas and result payloads across providers.
3. Fresh conversation per case; no cross-case context; case order randomized
   with a recorded seed.
4. Temperature 0 / minimum where supported; all sampling and reasoning settings
   recorded verbatim.
5. Same max-turn and token budgets across providers where controllable; hitting
   a cap is recorded in the result record, never silently truncated.
6. Pilot and smoke runs use only discarded candidates (pool minus frozen),
   never frozen cases.
7. Both arms run through one driver and one transcript format, and every
   run carries per-case evidence of its own tool environment (which tools
   were offered, whether the corpus backend connected, what the harness
   denied). A control case showing tool activity, or a treatment case
   whose backend never connected, invalidates that case rather than being
   absorbed into the aggregate; `runner/check_fairness.py` reports both
   from the committed artifacts.

### Recorded harness limitations (v1, Claude Code CLI)

- `--system-prompt` **appends to** rather than replaces the CLI's own
  preamble, so `system_prompt_sha256` covers the benchmark prompt bytes,
  not the whole system context. The preamble is identical across arms.
- The CLI exposes no temperature and no max-turn control; both are
  recorded as unset rather than as chosen values (§11 records settings
  verbatim).
- The CLI discovers instruction files (`CLAUDE.md`) from its working
  directory upward, and a sandboxed `HOME` does not stop it. Runs are
  therefore spawned inside an empty per-run sandbox directory. The
  Stage 5 pilots predate this fix and ran with development instructions
  in context; their answer content is not comparable to later runs.

### Matrix and repeats

3 providers × 2 conditions × 250 cases = 1,500 primary case-runs, one pass per
case (no repeats — owner decision 26). Stability check: a 30-case stratified
subset × **5** repeats per provider×condition to estimate run-to-run variance.
Aggregates are reported with bootstrap confidence intervals over cases,
absolute per condition plus the control−treatment delta.

The subset is drawn once, before any result exists — never from run output:
proportional largest-remainder allocation over the frozen category counts
(C1 6, C2 5, C3 4, C4 4, C5 2, C6 4, C7 3, C8 2), then a seeded
within-category sample over id-sorted rows (`lovspor.llhb.stability`, seed
recorded). The draw is committed as `dataset/frozen/llhb-v1-stability30.json`
with the frozen dataset checksum and its own subset checksum;
`runner/select_stability_subset.py` regenerates it byte-identically.
`run_arm.py --stability --repeat N` runs exactly that file and fails closed if
the subset no longer matches the frozen dataset it was drawn from.

Repeats reduce sampling noise as 1/√R and do not correct harness differences,
metric bias or scorer error, so the primary pass buys breadth (250 draws)
while the subset buys a measured variance estimate. If an observed delta turns
out small relative to its confidence interval, adding repeats to the primary
pass is a later, evidence-driven decision — not a precaution paid up front.

### Recorded metadata

Per run: everything in `schema/run_metadata.schema.json` — provider, exact model
ID, API version/date, system prompt (text + SHA-256), tool configuration,
sampling settings, timestamps, LLHB version, dataset checksum, lovspor commit,
lovverk commit, runner commit, evaluator version, case-order seed. A published
number that cannot be traced to this metadata must not be published.

Note on `semantic_search` in the treatment arm: query embedding is an external
OpenAI API call at run time. Retrieval results are captured in full in the tool
trace, so scoring is reproducible from the trace even though the embedding call
itself is an external dependency. This is a recorded limitation.

## 6. Scoring

All v1 metrics are deterministic; see `SCORING.md` for extraction rules, oracle
mapping, per-category criteria and metric formulas. No LLM-as-judge, no rubric,
no jurist (owner decision 2, consistent with the accepted Phase 3 doctrine in
`docs/publication-plan.md`).

## 7. Limitations (publish verbatim with any results)

- LLHB measures behaviour **on this benchmark**. It must not be described as
  proving that Lovspor eliminates hallucinations or guarantees legally correct
  answers.
- Lovspor is both the treatment and the source of ground truth. The oracle uses
  corpus facts (existence, text identity), not retrieval quality, and a sample
  of oracle verdicts is spot-checked against the Lovdata-derived source data;
  the residual self-reference is a stated limitation.
- Statutory text at one pinned corpus-retrieval-time state only; no claim about
  law in force on any date (ADR-0002).
- Only citation-level and quote-level hallucination is scored in v1. Paraphrase
  hallucination and unsupported interpretation (H5, full H6) are not measured —
  the existing docs' warning that the tool stack cannot prevent paraphrase
  hallucination applies to the benchmark's scope as well.
- Norwegian-language capability differences between providers are part of what
  is measured, not controlled away.
- Results are point-in-time: pinned corpus, recorded model IDs, recorded dates.
- Public-language rule (`docs/roadmap.md`): "grounded answers with verifiable
  citations" — never a guarantee that an LLM cannot make a mistake.
